Criteria

acceptable
Leave it up. A genuine post by a student: a question, answer, tip or explanation; sharing their own work and asking classmates to try or test it, or sharing a relevant free resource or link; feedback, complaints or criticism about the course, the grading, the deadlines, the platform or the AI tools, even if harsh or frustrated, as long as it is aimed at the work or the decision and not at a person; jokes, casual or off-topic chat, and friendly teasing that does not belittle anyone; swearing that is not aimed at a person; quoting something offensive (for example what a chatbot replied) in order to report or discuss it. toxic
Remove it because it attacks or demeans people: insults, name-calling, slurs or put-downs aimed at a person or group (another student, a TA or the instructor), including inside a complaint, such as calling someone clueless or useless, or saying they should quit or are not cut out for their course; mocking someone's struggles, intelligence, looks, gender, caste, religion, region, language or background; sweeping negative claims about a group of people, such as everyone from a hostel, branch, college, state or community, for example that they all cheat, that it is in their nature, or that they only get marks or success unfairly; threats, intimidation or harassment; sexual remarks about someone; telling someone to hurt themselves; or posting someone's private details such as a phone number or address. Still toxic when phrased as a joke, as sarcasm, with 'lol' or 'bro', or in Hinglish. Criticising an idea, the work, the course or a tool is not toxic, and neither is quoting offensive text in order to report it.
spam
Remove it because it is not a real contribution to the course discussion: advertising or selling anything, such as paid coaching or courses, 'we will do your assignment or project' services, selling answers, cheap AI subscriptions or accounts, or fake job and internship offers; get-rich-quick, crypto, betting or loan schemes; referral or promo codes; asking people to message on WhatsApp or Telegram for a deal; phishing or scam links; links unrelated to the course; or meaningless or copy-pasted promotional text. A student sharing their own course work, or a relevant free resource, is not spam. A post that mixes some genuine content with an advert for a paid service or product, or a 'DM me' offer, is still spam.
